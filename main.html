About the Project

This project documents the preparation and preliminary observation of a simple moisturizer-like prototype. It is designed to help students understand ingredient functions, careful measurement, manual mixing, basic quality observations and laboratory safety.

The work is an educational prototype, not a validated commercial cosmetic product. Appearance immediately after mixing cannot confirm long-term stability or microbiological safety.

Project Objectives

  • Understand the expected role of each ingredient.
  • Practice measuring and mixing a small batch.
  • Observe appearance, odour and texture.
  • Identify improvements needed for stability and safety.

Raw Materials & Their Functions

The report identifies four main ingredients. Their expected roles are described below.

🌱

Aloe Vera Gel

Forms the water-based gel portion and contributes to the formulation’s feel and hydration profile.

💧

Glycerin

A humectant that helps attract and retain moisture. Too much may make a formula feel sticky.

🥥

Coconut Oil

An emollient oil that can help soften the skin surface and reduce the feeling of dryness.

🟡

Vitamin E

Included for antioxidant and skin-conditioning properties. It does not replace a preservative.

Important: The supplied ingredient list does not include an emulsifier or preservative. The mixture may separate, and its microbiological safety has not been established.

Preparation Process

The reported method uses manual mixing and observation. Follow laboratory instructions and record actual quantities and conditions.

Prepare clean tools→ Measure ingredients→ Mix gel + glycerin→ Mix oil + vitamin E→ Combine gently→ Observe and label
Prepare the workspace

Clean the work surface and use clean, dry equipment. Wear suitable protective equipment.

Measure ingredients

Record ingredient names, brands and actual quantities used in the batch.

Prepare the portions

Mix aloe vera gel with glycerin. In a separate clean container, combine coconut oil and vitamin E.

Combine and inspect

Add the oil portion gradually while stirring gently. Record the sample’s appearance, colour, odour and texture.

Label the sample

Mark it as an educational prototype that has not been stability- or microbiologically tested.

Safety & Environment

  • Use clean tools and clearly label the sample.
  • Do not return unused ingredients to their original containers.
  • Dispose of the prototype according to laboratory instructions.
  • Do not pour bulk oil into drains.
  • Do not claim a shelf life without suitable testing.

Conclusion & Recommendations

The preparation of the Aloe Vera Moisturizer prototype was a useful practical activity that helped us understand the roles of aloe vera gel, glycerin, coconut oil and vitamin E. The sample was manually mixed and observed for its appearance, odour and texture.

The activity provided experience in ingredient measurement, mixing techniques, quality observation and laboratory safety. It also showed that combining ingredients does not automatically produce a stable cosmetic product.

Further work should use clearly specified cosmetic-grade ingredients and a qualified instructor’s guidance to select a suitable emulsifier and preservative. Appropriate stability, packaging-compatibility and microbiological tests are required before any shelf-life or routine-use claim can be made.

Overall, the project gave the team practical knowledge of basic cosmetic formulation and the importance of quality control and further testing.
